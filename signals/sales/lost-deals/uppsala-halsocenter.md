# Lost deal: Uppsala Hälsocenter

Three sites, about 2,600 members. Part-owned by a municipal foundation, which
turned out to be the whole story. Disqualified rather than lost.

## Why we lost, in their words

> "Vår IT-policy kräver att systemet driftas på vår egen infrastruktur eller hos
> en leverantör vi har ett personuppgiftsbiträdesavtal med som tillåter det."

Their IT policy requires the system to run on their own infrastructure.

## What actually happened

Two good meetings on the product. At the third, their IT manager joined and
asked where the data is held and whether it can run on-premise. I said no and
that it is not something we plan to offer. He said that ended it, and I think he
was right that it did.

They asked twice more whether there was a way around it: a dedicated instance, a
Swedish region, an escrow arrangement. I said no each time, which I believe was
the correct answer.

## What we did well

Qualified out reasonably quickly once the constraint was clear, rather than
building a proposal nobody could sign.

## What would have won it

Single-tenant or on-premise deployment. This is a stated non-goal, and I am not
arguing with it. I am recording that the requirement exists so that when the
next public-sector enquiry comes in, we recognise it at the first meeting rather
than the third.

## Honest note

We get two or three of these a year. Every one costs about three meetings before
someone mentions the constraint. A qualification question about ownership and IT
policy would save that.
