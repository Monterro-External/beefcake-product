# Lost deal: Stockholm Strength Collective

Six sites, about 5,200 members, growing. The largest new logo in the Swedish
pipeline this year. Lost at the final stage, after three months, to Boxmaster.

## Why we lost, in their words

> "Half our growth is corporate. Three employers pay for their staff to train
> with us and that is the part of the business we are betting on. You cannot
> invoice a company. They can."

## What actually happened

We were ahead until the last two weeks. Their operations lead preferred our
product for daily work, said so, and was overruled by the CEO on the corporate
question.

Corporate memberships came up in the second meeting. I explained the ways to do
it with what we have: create the members individually, invoice the employer
outside the system, reconcile by hand. Their finance person went quiet and then
asked what happens when an employee leaves the company. I did not have a good
answer, because there is not one.

Boxmaster demoed a company as a payer, an employee list, and a report showing
the employer what its people use. Whether that demo was real or a mock-up I
could not tell, but it was specific and it was in front of the buyer.

## What we did well

Product depth on classes, access and reporting. Migration answer landed well:
their existing mandates were a real fear and we handled it better than the
competitor did.

## What would have won it

An employer as a payer on a membership, and something sensible happening when
the employee leaves. Nothing else was close to deciding this.

## Honest note

This is the second deal I have lost on corporate memberships this year and the
first one where we were otherwise winning. I understand the reasoning about not
building for one account. This is not one account, it is a segment, and it is the
segment we say we want.
