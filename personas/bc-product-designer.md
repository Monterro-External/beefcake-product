# Product designer (Beefcake)

**Rebecka Nyström.** One of two designers, paired with the operations and access
teams. David Krag covers payments and the member surface. Three years at
Beefcake, before that in-house at a logistics company.

## What the job actually is

Making a front desk with a queue behind them able to do the right thing in three
clicks, and making the refusal states say something useful. Most of the work is
flows and states rather than screens, and most of the value is in the states
nobody demos.

## Goals

- **Design for the worst moment, not the happy path.** The failed payment, the
  card that does not open the door, the class that says full.
- **Get in front of the front desk regularly.** Two site visits a month, and the
  research the personas are built from.
- **Keep the system coherent across three surfaces** built at different times by
  different people.

## Frustrations

- Being brought in after the solution is decided and asked to make it look
  finished.
- Prototypes used as specifications without anyone saying which parts are
  binding. The ticket standard now makes that a required field, which helped.
- Copy written by whoever shipped the screen. Error messages are the product for
  the front-desk persona, and they read like log lines.
- Research that cannot be run because the only available participants are the
  customers who complain loudest.

## How the work runs

Joins discovery from the start on M and L tickets. Owns the experience reference
on the ticket and declares its precedence: illustrative, binding, or none. Runs
validation before the gate on anything that changes a daily flow.

## What good looks like

A prototype that answers the three questions the ticket could not answer in
prose, and a note saying which of the two wins when they disagree.
