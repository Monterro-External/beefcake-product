# Front desk

**Elias, 24.** Works the desk at Gymmet Vasastan, a single-site gym in Stockholm
with 520 members. Studies part time. Has been there fourteen months, which makes
him the second most experienced person on the rota.

Segment: all three. ICP fit: universal. There are more people in this role using
Beefcake than in any other, and the product principle that names them exists
because they are the least likely to be in the room.

## What he is actually responsible for

Whoever is standing in front of him. Check-ins, selling a membership, freezing
one, cancelling one, finding out why a card does not open the turnstile, and
answering the phone while doing all of it. He has a queue behind the person he is
talking to for most of the day.

## Goals

- **Finish the conversation.** Every task is measured by whether the person in
  front of him leaves satisfied within about ninety seconds.
- **Not be the one who says "I don't know".** He will invent an explanation
  rather than admit the system will not tell him, and that invention becomes
  what the member believes.
- **Not make a mistake with someone's money.** Freezing the wrong month or
  cancelling with the wrong notice date comes back to him personally.

## Frustrations

- Being told something is not possible without being told what is. A greyed-out
  button with no reason is the single most common complaint in this role.
- Screens that require him to know a member's exact spelling. Members give him
  their first name and a vague gesture.
- Anything that takes more than three clicks while somebody watches.
- Being the last to know. Staff hear about product changes from members.

## How he uses Beefcake

All day, on a shared desktop at the desk, logged in as a shared front-desk user
with a colleague's session open in another tab. Member search, member profile,
freeze, plan change, notice, sell a membership, access log. He never opens
reports and does not have rights to settings.

He keeps a paper note by the till with the three things the system will not let
him do, and what to tell members instead.

## What he says

> "I say the payment did not go through and they should check their bank. I do
> not actually know if that is true, but it is what usually works."

> "If it is going to say no, it should say no before I have typed everything in."

## What this means for us

- Error and refusal states are the primary surface for this persona. What the
  system says when it declines is the product.
- Speed matters in a specific way: the number of decisions he must make, rather
  than the page load.
- The paper note by the till is a bug list we do not receive. Support tickets
  from this role are the small fraction of problems that survived long enough to
  be worth reporting.
