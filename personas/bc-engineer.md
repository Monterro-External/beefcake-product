# Engineer (Beefcake)

**Nour Haddad.** Senior engineer on the payments team, one of eighteen engineers
in four teams. Four years at Beefcake and the person most likely to know why a
piece of billing code looks the way it does.

Engineering is led by Elin Sandberg. The other teams cover operations and access,
member experience, and platform.

## What the job actually is

Receiving a Product Ticket, interrogating it against the codebase, turning it
into a Spec and then into work, and living with the result at 07:00 when a
payment run has not finished.

## Goals

- **Understand the problem behind the request.** The ticket that says what a
  customer experiences leaves room to solve it properly. The ticket that
  specifies a solution usually specifies the second-best one.
- **Not be surprised at the end.** Localisation, permissions, what happens to
  existing data, what the customer is told when it fails. These are product
  decisions and they arrive late or not at all.
- **Keep the fleet safe.** One process, 340 operators, and a wrong default is an
  incident everywhere at once.

## Frustrations

- Tickets that describe a screen instead of a problem, then change when the
  screen turns out to be wrong.
- "It should be fast." Fast is a number or it is nothing.
- Regression risk stated as a hope. The S-tier regression clause exists because
  of this and it is the single most useful line product writes.
- Being asked for an estimate on a ticket with three open questions.

## What is asked of product

Answers to the questions only product can answer: which roles, which plans,
what happens to data that already exists, how long it is kept, what the audit
trail shows, which language, whether it is admin-facing or member-facing, what
the customer sees when it fails, and which events to emit. These come back into
the ticket as product decisions, and that round trip is expected.

## What good looks like

A ticket that can be turned into EARS requirements without a meeting, and whose
acceptance scenarios already contain the error case.
