# Ideal customer profile

Owner: Malin Ohlsson (CPO). Written 2026-02-04 from the 2025 win/loss review,
the segment margin analysis and twelve customer interviews. Reviewed 2026-07-02.

## The profile

**A multi-site gym operator with 800 to 6,000 members, running recurring
memberships and a class schedule, taking payment through us, in Sweden or
Norway.**

The four qualifying facts, in the order they matter:

1. **Recurring payments run through Beefcake Pay.** Payment volume is a revenue
   line and the reason our per-site price can stay competitive. An operator who
   keeps their own payment provider is a seat customer with a lower ceiling and
   a weaker attachment.
2. **More than one site.** Multi-site is where the product does something a
   spreadsheet cannot: shared plans, cross-site access, aggregated reporting.
   It is also where switching costs become real.
3. **A class schedule that matters.** Classes drive attendance, attendance drives
   retention for the operator, and the waitlist is the feature staff use daily.
   An operator running a pure gym floor uses a third of the product.
4. **Somebody whose job includes reading a report.** Where nobody reads a report,
   nobody notices the value we create, and the renewal conversation is about
   price.

Fit by segment, as it actually is rather than as we would like it:

| Segment | Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mid, 2 to 5 sites | **Best** | All four qualifying facts, ~35% of ARR, best gross margin, lowest support cost per krona |
| Key accounts, 6 to 15 sites | **Strategic** | Excellent revenue, real capability gaps, highest cost to serve, and the segment we lose |
| Long tail, single site | **Adequate** | Pays, complains, rarely leaves. Profitable in aggregate and no kind of growth engine |

## Who is deliberately not ICP

- **Studios under 150 members.** Support cost approaches ARR, and Gymly is
  cheaper and good enough. We do not build for them and we do not discount for
  them.
- **Franchise groups needing per-franchisee billing entities.** Each franchisee
  is a separate legal payee with separate settlement. That is a different
  product and it collides with how tenants and payouts work.
- **Personal-training studios and boutique concepts** whose core need is
  delivering and selling training rather than operating a membership base.
- **Operators outside the Nordics.** Until Denmark is proven, this includes
  every enquiry from Germany, the Netherlands and the UK, of which we get two or
  three a month.
- **Public-sector and municipal facilities**, where procurement requires
  on-premise deployment or data residency guarantees we do not offer.

Not ICP does not mean not a customer. About 60 of our current 340 operators sit
outside the profile above, and they are welcome. It means they do not shape the
roadmap and they do not justify a non-goal being reopened.

## What an ICP operator's year looks like

January brings a sign-up surge and a freeze surge in the same month. Summer
empties the schedule and fills the freeze queue. The payment run goes out on the
25th and the following three days are when the front desk hears about money. A
site manager plans the schedule about six weeks ahead. The owner looks at churn
and revenue monthly, and at nothing at all in the weeks between.

Buying happens after a bad experience with the incumbent, most often a billing
one, and the buying committee is the owner plus whoever will have to run the
migration. The question that decides the deal is what happens to their existing
mandates.

## How we use this

- Prioritisation weights evidence from mid and key accounts above the long tail
  when they conflict, and says so in the ticket rather than pretending the
  evidence was equal.
- A request from outside the profile needs a reason beyond the request itself.
- Sales qualification uses the four facts above, in that order.
