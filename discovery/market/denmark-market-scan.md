# Market scan: Denmark

Written to answer one question: is Denmark a market we enter properly, or a
market we stop selling into? Both are acceptable answers. The current position,
one customer and no local payment method, is not.

## Situation

We have one Danish operator, Fitness Nørrebro, 520 members, acquired
opportunistically in 2024. Danish pipeline over the last year: eleven qualified
conversations, two proposals, one win, and that win was the existing customer's
second site, which did not happen.

Denmark is roughly 1,100 commercial gym operators. Our addressable slice, using
the same ICP filter as Sweden, is perhaps 260.

## Findings

### 1. MobilePay is not a preference

MobilePay is used by around 95% of Danish adults and is the default way to pay a
person or a small business. Every Danish conversation we have had opened with it.
Two prospects stopped the conversation at the answer.

It is not only about the recurring payment. Danish operators sign members up at
the desk and take the first payment immediately, exactly as Norwegian ones do.

### 2. The invoice is a real requirement, not an accountant's preference

Danish receipts must show the VAT rate and amount, and invoice numbers must run
consecutively per calendar year. Our only Danish customer's accountant rebuilds
this from the CSV export every quarter and has asked about it every quarter for
two years.

This is a smaller piece of work than MobilePay and it is the one that would make
an existing customer stop working around us.

### 3. The competition is local and adequate

Two Danish vendors hold most of the mid market. Neither is strong on multi-site
or access hardware. Both have MobilePay and Danish invoicing, which means the
comparison starts at the point where we are strongest and they are weakest, if we
can get to it.

Gymly is present and growing in Denmark, at the small end.

### 4. Support and language

Danish operators accept English support more readily than Norwegian or Finnish
ones do, and our support already covers the Danish queue in Danish. This is not
a barrier.

### 5. Our own credibility

Both Danish prospects asked how many Danish customers we have. The answer, one,
ended one conversation on its own. This is the compounding problem in a small
market: the first three customers cost far more than the next thirty.

## Implications

| Finding | If we enter | If we exit |
|---|---|---|
| MobilePay | Required before any Danish selling resumes | Not needed |
| Danish invoicing | Required, and required anyway for the customer we have | Still required for one customer |
| Local competition | Beatable on multi-site and access | Irrelevant |
| Credibility | Needs three or four reference customers, which needs a year | Solved by leaving |
| Support | Ready | Ready |

The asymmetry: the invoice work is needed whether we enter or exit, because we
have a Danish customer either way. MobilePay is the entry decision.

## The three questions only evidence can answer

1. **Would MobilePay convert our pipeline?** Eleven conversations, two lost on
   it explicitly. That is suggestive rather than conclusive, and the honest test
   is to build it and see, which is exactly the kind of bet the gate exists to
   scrutinise.
2. **Is the Danish mid market big enough to matter?** 260 addressable operators
   at our average mid ARR is a theoretical 30 MSEK. Reaching 5% of it in three
   years would be 1.5 MSEK, which is not a strategy on its own.
3. **What does entering cost the Norwegian plan?** Vipps and MobilePay are
   similar work in the same area, and doing both is not twice the cost of one.
   Doing Denmark first would be.

## Recommendation to the gate

Sequence, do not choose: Norway first because the revenue is already there and
the gap is one method, then Denmark as an explicit test with a kill criterion. Do
the Danish invoice work regardless, because we have a customer.

If the gate does not fund Denmark, say so publicly and stop taking Danish inbound.
The current position wastes a good salesperson's time and gives Danish operators
a poor impression of us.
