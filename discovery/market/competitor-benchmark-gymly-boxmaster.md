# Competitor benchmark: Gymly, Boxmaster

Written for the prioritisation gate, not for sales enablement. Where we have
only seen a competitor's screen through a prospect's description, it says so.

## One-pagers

### Gymly

Nordic, Helsinki-based, roughly our age, cheaper by 30 to 45% at single-site
pricing. Strong on booking and on a light, fast interface. Weak on payments:
they integrate a PSP rather than owning the payment relationship, so their
operators reconcile two systems.

Where they beat us: price, sign-up speed at the desk, and Vipps and MobilePay,
which they have had for two years.

Where we beat them: multi-site, access hardware, reporting, and anything where
the operator's own money is involved.

Who they win: single-site gyms under about 800 members. We lost four of those
this year and all four were correct losses.

### Boxmaster

Global, US-owned, sold through a partner in the Nordics. Expensive, long
implementations, and a feature list that covers every question a chain asks.
Corporate memberships, employer portals, franchise structures, revenue reporting
that a CFO recognises.

Where they beat us: corporate memberships, reporting depth, and the confidence
that comes from having done it before at scale.

Where we beat them: price, time to live, local payment methods, local support in
the local language, and the daily experience of the people at the desk. This last
point is consistent and it is not enough on its own: in both deals we lost to
them this year, the operations lead preferred us and was overruled.

### Spreadsheets and the incumbent nobody names

Still the real competitor under about 300 members. Free, understood, and owned by
the person who built it. We do not compete here and should not.

## On the jobs our ICP actually has

| Job | Us | Gymly | Boxmaster |
|---|---|---|---|
| Take a recurring payment reliably in SE, NO, FI, DK | Strong, and it is our moat | Adequate, PSP-dependent | Strong, expensive |
| Sign a member up at the desk in two minutes | Adequate in SE, weak in NO and DK | Strong everywhere | Weak, built for a sales office |
| Run a class schedule with a waitlist | Strong | Strong | Adequate |
| Access hardware that works offline | Strong, two vendors | Weak, one vendor | Strong, many vendors |
| Report across sites | Adequate, and it degrades at key-account volume | Weak | Strong |
| Invoice an employer for its staff | **Absent** | Absent | Strong |
| Migrate existing mandates without re-signing members | Strong, and undersold | Unknown | Adequate, done by services |

The two rows that decide deals are the two we do not lead: sign-up speed in
Norway and Denmark, and invoicing an employer.

## What the losses actually say

Nine debriefs. Grouped by what would have changed the outcome:

- **A payment method** (4). All Norwegian or Danish. Gymly took three of them.
- **Corporate or employer-paid memberships** (2). Both to Boxmaster, both deals
  we were otherwise winning.
- **Price** (2). Both single-site, both outside the ICP.
- **Deployment policy** (1). Public sector, correctly disqualified.

Nobody has left us or refused us because of classes, access, migration or the
daily desk experience. Those are the things we spend most of our capacity on.

## Positioning that survives contact with a prospect

- Against Gymly: **your members' money is our product, not an integration.** Then
  the migration procedure, which they cannot match.
- Against Boxmaster: **live in six weeks, in your language, at a third of the
  price**, and let their implementation timeline do the rest of the work.

Neither line works in Norway until the sign-up question is answered, because the
conversation ends before positioning starts.

## What we could not find out

- Whether Boxmaster's employer portal is a real product or a demo environment.
  Two prospects described it; neither could show us.
- Gymly's churn. Their public customer count grew 40% year on year, which is
  either growth or a lot of onboarding.
- Whether Gymly is moving up into the mid segment. Their pricing page gained a
  multi-site tier in February, which is a signal and not evidence.
