# Riikka Salo, owner, Kuntoklubi Kallio

Single site in Helsinki, 780 members, six staff. Owner since 2019. Her background
is in software testing, which shows in everything below and makes her
unrepresentative of the long tail in exactly one useful way: she can describe
what she sees precisely.

## Context

An hour, most of it with the product open. She had prepared a list. We did not
ask her to.

## What we heard

On how she works:

> "I look every day. I know that is not normal. My previous job was testing
> software and I cannot stop."

On the payment run:

> "The 25th and then the three days after. That is when the desk hears about
> money. I look at the failed list on the 26th."

On what the failed list gives her:

> "A code. `insufficient_funds`, fine, I know what that is. `technical`, what am
> I supposed to do with `technical`? I have thirty of those over the year and I
> have never known what any of them were."

On reporting a problem to us:

> "I write it properly. Steps, what I expected, what happened, the member number.
> Then I get an answer that reads like nobody read it."

> "So now I write it properly and I expect nothing. I still write it properly,
> because one day someone will read it."

On churn:

> "The ones who stop paying and never say anything. Those are the ones I want.
> They are not angry, they just drift. If I knew a month earlier I could phone
> them."

On the class schedule and the door, asked directly:

> "The schedule is fine. The door is fine. Once or twice a month something odd
> in the access log, late in the evening, but nothing a member has complained
> about, so I have not reported it."

## Surprises

- She has thirty unexplained `technical` failure codes a year and has never
  raised it, because each individual one is too small to report.
- Her support tickets are the highest quality we receive and she believes they
  are ignored. Checking the queue: several are closed with a generic reply.
- She notices things and does not report them when no member has complained.
  The threshold for reporting is member pain rather than observation.

## Implications

- Failure codes are shown raw to the operator and at least one of them is
  meaningless at the receiving end. That is a small, cheap, high-frequency
  improvement in the payments area.
- Our highest-signal reporter has been trained by our own responses to expect
  nothing. That is a support-process problem with product consequences: we are
  losing the best inputs we get.
- Silent churn is the owner's stated priority in her own words, and it is the
  same population as the involuntary churn in the OKR, seen from the other side.

## Follow-ups

- Pull her last ten tickets and read how they were handled. Done: eight closed
  with a template reply, two escalated.
- Ask her to send anything odd from the access log even without a member
  complaint. She agreed.
