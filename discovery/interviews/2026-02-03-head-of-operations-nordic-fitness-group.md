# Petra Lindgren, head of operations, Nordic Fitness Group

Fifteen sites across Sweden and Norway, about 22,000 members. Petra runs
operations for all of them and is the person every site escalates to. She is our
main day-to-day contact and the person who will write the internal
recommendation about the renewal.

## Context

At their head office, with Marte present as CSM. A commercial conversation was
happening in parallel elsewhere in the building, which we knew and she knew.
Notes reviewed by Marte before filing.

## What we heard

On the escalation path:

> "A site tells me. I decide whether it is a Beefcake problem. If it is, I send
> it to Marte. About one a week, maybe more after the payment run."

On what happens then:

> "Sometimes it is fixed. Usually I hear that it has been passed on. I have
> stopped telling the sites that I have reported it, because then they ask me
> what happened."

On reports:

> "I run the member report for the board every month. It takes long enough that I
> start it and go and do something else. Sometimes it does not finish at all and
> I run it again in the evening when it is quieter."

Asked whether she had reported that:

> "I mentioned it. I do not remember if I sent a ticket. It is not broken, it is
> just slow, and I have a workaround, which is to do it in the evening."

On corporate memberships, which she raised herself:

> "This is the thing I need. Our biggest three corporate customers want to pay
> for their staff in one invoice, and I have to do it in a spreadsheet and enter
> the members by hand. Boxmaster has it."

> "I am not threatening you. I am telling you that when the renewal comes I have
> to write down what we can and cannot do, and this goes in the second column."

On what good would look like:

> "The company pays, the employee is a member, the employee leaves the company
> and the membership does something sensible. I do not care how."

On the relationship:

> "You are fine. Support is slow and the reports are slow and everything else is
> fine. If someone asked me today I would say we are staying."

## Surprises

- The slow report is a workaround rather than a complaint. She has never filed
  it because it completes eventually, which means our support queue understates
  this by design.
- She describes the corporate membership need without a solution, which is
  unusual at this level and makes it a good candidate for real discovery.
- Her stated position on the renewal is positive, and the competitor evaluation
  is happening anyway. Both things are true and the second one is the one that
  ends up in a spreadsheet.

## Implications

- Key-account pain arrives as workarounds and not as tickets. Anything measured
  by ticket volume systematically under-weights the segment with 35% of ARR.
- Corporate memberships is a data-model question dressed as a billing request,
  and the interesting part is her last sentence about what happens when the
  employee leaves.
- The escalation path loses fidelity twice: site to head office, head office to
  CSM. What reaches us is a summary of a summary, which is why it so often
  reads as vague.

## Follow-ups

- Ask the sites directly rather than through head office. Sofia Palm's interview
  in round A was worth more than three escalation emails.
- Corporate memberships needs its own discovery. Not a ticket yet.
- Check how long her member report actually takes at their volume. Nobody has
  measured it.
