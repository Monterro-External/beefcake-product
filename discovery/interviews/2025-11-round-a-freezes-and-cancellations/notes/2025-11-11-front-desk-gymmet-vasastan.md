# Elias Berg, front desk, Gymmet Vasastan

Single site, 520 members, five people on the rota. Elias has worked here fourteen
months and studies alongside it. He is the second most experienced person at the
desk, which says something about the role.

## Context

Interviewed standing at the desk during a quiet hour, interrupted six times. We
kept the recorder running through the interruptions with his permission, and the
interruptions were more informative than the answers.

## What we heard

On the last freeze request, twenty minutes before we arrived:

> "She is going to Thailand. I put in the dates she said. She asked when the
> money stops and I said next month, which I think is right."

**Observed.** It was not right for her case, and he did not check. He was
confident and pleasant and the member left happy. This is the failure mode: the
answer is delivered with total confidence and nobody finds out for six weeks.

On what he does when the system says no:

> "There is a note here." (A folded paper by the till.) "Three things it will not
> let me do and what to say instead. My colleague wrote it before me and I added
> one."

**Observed.** The note listed: a member with an unpaid charge cannot change plan,
what to say when a card does not open the door on a Sunday, and how to handle
someone who wants to freeze a membership that is already frozen. All three are
places where the product refuses without explaining.

On payments:

> "I say it did not go through and they should check with their bank. I do not
> actually know if that is true. It is what my manager told me to say."

On what he can see:

> "I can see it says failed. I cannot see why. So I say check with your bank,
> because usually it is the bank."

On the worst part of the job:

> "When they are angry and I cannot tell them anything. I would rather it said
> something bad than nothing."

## Surprises

- The paper note by the till is a defect list that never reaches us. Two of the
  three items have never appeared in a support ticket from this tenant.
- He has never been trained on the product. He was trained by the colleague who
  had the shift before him, who was trained the same way.
- He was more upset by not being able to explain than by the underlying refusal.

## Implications

- Refusal states are the front desk's primary surface and they are currently
  bare. What the product says when it declines is doing more work than most
  features we ship.
- Front-desk staff will fabricate an explanation rather than say they do not
  know, and that fabrication becomes what the member believes and repeats.
- Support ticket volume from the long tail understates the problem badly.
  Anything with a workaround written on a piece of paper never gets reported.

## Follow-ups

- Photograph the paper note at every site visit from now on. Two so far.
- Check what the product tells a front-desk user when a plan change is blocked
  by an unpaid charge. Nothing useful, as far as we can see.
