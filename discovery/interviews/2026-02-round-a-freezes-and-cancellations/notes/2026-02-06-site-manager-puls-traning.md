# Nadia Ohlin, site manager, Puls Träning Kista

About 600 members on her site, five staff, twenty-eight classes a week. Four
years in the role, previously front desk at the same site.

## Context

Interviewed at the desk between the morning and lunch peaks. She handled two
members mid-conversation and narrated what she was doing, which was the most
useful part of the hour.

## What we heard

On the freeze conversation:

> "Nobody says freeze. They say they are going away, or they are injured, or
> money is tight this month. My job is to work out which one it is, because the
> injured ones come back and the money ones do not."

On the rules:

> "Officially two months. I do three if I like them, and I do not tell Marcus."

**Observed.** She froze a member for the period the member asked for, then
wrote the member's name and the return date on a paper note stuck to the monitor.
Asked why, she said she wants to phone them the week before they come back,
"because that is when you keep them".

On what the system does not tell her:

> "I cannot see who is coming back this month. I have the notes on the screen
> here. If I lose these notes I lose those members."

**Observed.** The paper notes on the monitor were eleven names. She has been
doing this for four years.

On cancellations:

> "The notice period is where I get into trouble. They give notice on the 3rd
> and they think they stop paying at the end of the month, and they do not,
> because thirty days is thirty days. I explain it about twice a week."

On exceptions at Kista:

> "I decide. If the member is upset and it is small, I fix it and move on. Marcus
> would rather I did that than escalate everything, but nobody has written that
> down."

## Surprises

- The single highest-value retention behaviour at this site is a paper note. The
  product has no concept of a member returning from a freeze.
- She routinely exceeds the tenant's configured maximum freeze months, and the
  system lets her, which means the setting is not enforcing what the owner
  thinks it is enforcing.
- Explaining the notice period is a twice-weekly conversation. It is one of the
  most frequent things staff do and there is nothing in the product to help.

## Implications

- Returning-from-freeze is a moment nobody owns in the product. That is an
  opportunity for the retention outcome and not a request anyone has filed.
- The gap between the notice rule and the member's expectation is a copy and
  communication problem before it is a rules problem.
- Site-level discretion is real and undocumented. Any change to freeze rules
  has to survive it.

## Follow-ups

- Count how many freezes across the fleet exceed the tenant's `max_freeze_months`.
  If it is common at more tenants, the setting is decorative.
- Ask the front desk persona what they say about notice periods. Booked 11-11.
