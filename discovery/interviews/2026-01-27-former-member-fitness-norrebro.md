# Camilla, former member, Fitness Nørrebro

Member for eleven months, stopped in November 2025. Went to the gym twice a week,
mostly on her own, occasionally to a class. The only former member in either
round, which is a weakness of both.

## Context

Recruited by the operator from a list of memberships that lapsed rather than
being cancelled. She agreed readily and was more willing to be critical than any
current member we spoke to, which is the point.

## What we heard

On why she left:

> "I did not leave. I stopped paying. There is a difference, I think, from your
> side." [Original: "Jeg stoppede bare med at betale."]

On how it happened:

> "The payment failed in October. I meant to fix it. Then there was a message,
> and another one, and by then it felt like a decision had been made."

> "It was easier to let it stop than to go in and sort it out."

On whether she would have stayed:

> "Probably? I liked it there. If someone had phoned me I would have fixed it in
> two minutes. Nobody phoned."

On what would have kept her:

> "If I could have paused it. I had a bad month, that is all it was. Cancelling
> felt final and paying felt impossible, so I did nothing."

Asked whether she knew she could freeze:

> "No. Is that a thing? Nobody told me."

On the gym itself:

> "The gym was fine. This is not really about the gym."

## Surprises

- She does not experience herself as having cancelled. From her side she stopped
  paying, and the difference matters: nobody at the gym registered a decision
  they could have argued with.
- She did not know freezing existed. The freeze is a front-desk transaction and
  she never asked the front desk anything.
- Her account of the sequence is that the dunning messages made the outcome feel
  decided for her. That is the opposite of their intent.

## Implications

- The largest churn population we have is people who did not choose to leave,
  and there is currently no product moment that offers them anything other than
  paying or stopping.
- A member in financial difficulty has one route to a freeze: ask a person at a
  desk, in a gym, in the middle of the day. That is the wrong route for exactly
  the people who need it.
- Combining this with the failed-payment cohort in the platform data is the next
  analysis to run, and it will need one careful hand: the members in that cohort
  never filed a complaint, so no amount of reading support tickets would have
  found this.
